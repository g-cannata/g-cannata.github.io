## Policy, knowledge and learning

This has been the core axis of my work in the past few years. I have been trying to understand what is the role of knowledge in policymaking – be it scientific knowledge, impact assessment reports, professional know-how or even just personal knowledge. Learning, more specifically <em>policy learning</em>, has been the lens I have used to wrap my head around this puzzle. The gist is that we can understand policymaking as a process wherby actors of different sorts engage in constant sense-making and learn (or fail to do so) how to get a hold over ever more complex problems. 

This is not to say that power doesn't matter. To the contrary, learning is crcucially entangled with it. But looking at how people, politicians, bureaucrats and interested parties of sort try to figure out what the best course of action is, for them, is a powerful analytical device. 

## At the science-policy interface

Research, however, often felt somewhat disconnected from what was happening on the ground. In parallel with my theoretical ramblings, I recently tried to work in the direction of shorten what I perceive as gap between my – and others' – research and the ways in which policies are made. I had the chance to explore this idea of science-for-policy initatives so collaborating with the European Commission's Joint Research Centre (JRC), and I am now looking for new avenues where to pursue this idea of making science and policy talk to each other – and, perhaps, realise that they are not as separate as people seem to suggest.
